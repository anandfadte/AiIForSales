# Deliver & Debrief

> Run /human, present the brief, then see how the activity was designed. (~7 minutes)

## research_mode
Executive Communication | Distilling intelligence into a brief the account exec will read in 2 minutes and act on

## context

A pre-meeting brief is not a research dump. It is a document designed to change how your account team walks into the room. The VP has 2 minutes to read it. Everything that does not change their behaviour in the meeting does not belong in it. Run /human last — it will cut the noise and make it sound like it was written by someone who has done this fifty times.

## tools_this_phase
/human
Always the last skill before you present.

## do_this

1. Run /human on your draft brief. Prompt: "This is a pre-meeting intelligence brief for a VP of Operations meeting in 5 days. The reader is an enterprise account executive with 2 minutes before a meeting. Remove everything that does not change how they behave in the room. Make it sound like it was written by someone who has prepared 50 of these briefings."
2. Present the brief. The presenter names one specific thing they would do differently in the meeting based on what this brief surfaced.
3. Debrief as a group: where did the AI surface something you would not have found manually? Where did your judgment override the AI output? Was that the right call?

## prompting_guideline

/human works best when you tell it the reader and the standard. Be direct: "The reader needs this in 2 minutes before a VP meeting. Remove every piece of information that does not change how the account exec behaves in the room. If it is a fact without an action, cut it."

## checkpoint

Could your account exec walk into the VP meeting tomorrow using only this brief? If they would still need to do more research, the brief has not finished the job. That is the standard.

## reveal_distractors

**The LinkedIn post.** The VP's LinkedIn profile showed a post about "digital transformation" from three months ago. This was the most visible piece of information about her and was deliberately placed first in the document pack for this activity. Groups that anchored on digital transformation missed the operational efficiency signal in the quarterly report — a 15% increase in reported process exceptions over 60 days. That is the VP's real problem. Digital transformation is the narrative she uses externally. Process exceptions are what she is measured on internally.

## reveal_quirks

**The reporting line had changed.** The document included a network org chart from an internal slide. The VP of Operations is listed as reporting directly to the CFO, not the CEO as your CRM notes suggest. This was updated following a merger seven months ago. Any brief that uses the old reporting structure assumes a different set of priorities — the CFO relationship means cost and efficiency metrics carry more weight than transformation narratives right now.

## reveal_insight_path

Meridian Health's most significant operational signal was in their most recent earnings call transcript: they are targeting a 20% reduction in clinical workflow friction as a board-level KPI this fiscal year. The VP of Ops owns that number. Groups that ran /earnings-agent found this metric; groups that only ran web search found the press release headline but not the specific KPI. The meeting opener that lands: "We noticed clinical workflow friction is a board KPI this year — we have addressed exactly that for two comparable health networks and would like to show you what that looked like."

## reveal_takeaway

Before your next significant client meeting, run this 5-phase sequence with your AI tool. The 20 minutes you spend on live intelligence and document probe will change more about how you run the meeting than any amount of CRM review. The gap between what your CRM holds and what your AI tool can surface from public sources in real time is where most account teams leave opportunity.
