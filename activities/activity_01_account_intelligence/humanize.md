# Polish
> Polish everything into executive-grade intelligence. Run /human last, always. (~4 min)

## context

/human is the last step in every activity. It transforms the raw compound intelligence — /account-research profile and /earnings-agent financial analysis — into language a VP of Operations would take seriously.

Do NOT run /human before the other phases are complete. The polish is only as good as the intelligence underneath it.

## prompt
/human Take everything in this conversation — the /account-research account profile and the /earnings-agent financial intelligence brief — and polish it into a pre-meeting intelligence pack for my 30-minute meeting with Alex Thornton, VP Operations at Barclays UK Retail Banking.

Requirements:
1. EXECUTIVE TONE — this should read like a senior banking operations advisor prepared it, not an AI
2. NO JARGON — replace consulting-speak with plain, confident language
3. DATA-BACKED — every claim supported by a specific number from our research
4. PERSONALIZED — reference Barclays' efficiency programme and what it means for Thornton's operational priorities
5. ACTIONABLE — not just information, but what to DO with it in the meeting

Structure:
- Executive Summary (3 sentences — the 'why this meeting matters')
- Company Snapshot (Barclays current state with key metrics)
- Financial Case (the numbers that matter to a VP Operations)
- 5 Opening Talking Points (what to say in the first 5 minutes)
- Pain-to-Solution Map (their operational challenges → Genpact capabilities)
- Recommended Meeting Agenda (how to structure the 30 minutes)
- Risk Factors (what could go wrong, how to handle it)

Total: 800 words. Every sentence must earn its place. If Alex Thornton read this, he should think 'this person understands banking operations, not just what they're selling.'

## tip

Read the /human output aloud. If you stumble over a sentence or it sounds like it was written by a committee, it still needs work. The test: would a senior banking operations advisor be proud to put their name on this?

## reveal_good

- Intelligence pack references Barclays' specific efficiency programme — not generic UK banking facts
- Financial insights are precise — actual cost efficiency ratio, operational metrics from their last earnings
- Executive brief is personalized — references Thornton's specific mandate and the pressure he is under
- Financial case uses THEIR numbers, not industry averages — Barclays-specific ratios and targets
- /human polish is evident — executive tone, no AI-speak, reads like a senior advisor wrote it
- Both intelligence layers are present — account profile (/account-research) and financial signals (/earnings-agent)
- Meeting agenda is specific — not 'discuss opportunities' but 'reference efficiency ratio in minute 3, pivot to Genpact operational transformation'

## reveal_takeaway

Ask yourself: if Alex Thornton read this pack, would he take the meeting seriously or think 'another vendor who Googled us'? The difference is in the depth — /account-research alone gives you structure. Add /earnings-agent and you have financial context. Add /human and you have executive credibility. Both plugin outputs together, polished into one voice, is what makes the first 5 minutes count.
