## context

## prompt
/human Polish the entire business case into CFO-ready language for Rebecca Torres. She's former Nordstrom VP Finance — she reads financial statements for breakfast.

Rules:
- Every sentence must contain or reference a number
- No consulting cliches: no 'unlock value,' 'synergies,' 'transformation journey'
- Speak CFO: IRR, NPV, payback, basis points, working capital, free cash flow
- Reference HER words from the earnings call ('entirely operational,' 'embarrassingly manual,' 'ROI in year one')
- Structure for a 30-minute meeting: first 5 minutes = hook with her own numbers, next 15 = the model, last 10 = discussion

Make this sound like Goldman Sachs prepared it, not a consulting firm. 800 words.

## reveal_good

- Business case uses Cascade's OWN numbers — DSO 44, error rate 6.8%, margin 4.2%, not generic benchmarks
- CFO quotes from investor presentation are woven into the pitch — 'as you said, margin compression is entirely operational'
- ROI model shows year-one payback — Torres explicitly required this
- Web research adds real-time context — latest analyst sentiment, peer comparison data
- /human output reads like financial advisory, not consulting — Goldman tone, not McKinsey tone
- All 4 layers tagged — you can trace every number to its source
- 'Why now' argument quantifies cost of waiting — every month of delay in $M impact

## reveal_takeaway

Rebecca Torres will stress-test every number. If you used a benchmark where you could have used HER number, she'll notice. The compound test: does your business case reference (1) live financial data from web search, (2) her exact words from the investor presentation, (3) your CRM relationship context, AND (4) Genpact-specific solution data from skills? If any layer is missing, the pitch has a gap she'll find.
