# Scenario Brief
> Frame the expansion as a strategic conversation, not a vendor pitch. (~3 min)

## scenario

You're a Client Partner managing Genpact's relationship with **Horizon Energy Solutions** — a $7.3B utility company. Your existing engagement ($8M/year, finance & accounting operations) has been solid for 3 years. The QBR is in 2 weeks, and your SVP wants you to pitch expansion into **3 new areas**: IT operations, customer service transformation, and supply chain optimization.

Horizon's CEO, **Maria Santos**, will attend the QBR for the first time. She said: *"I'm open-minded but skeptical. Show me you understand our industry challenges, not just your service catalog."*

**Current F&A results:** 99.5% accuracy, $4.1M savings.

## context

This is the longest skill chain in the program — 4 skills + /human. The key is that each skill must build on the previous one. /earnings-agent sets the financial frame. /account-research adds account intelligence. /cxo-strategic-brief translates to CEO language. /solution-assistant maps specific solutions. /human polishes for Maria Santos.

**Time: 25 minutes.** Start the timer — this is a longer chain.

## do_this

1. The CEO will decide in the first 3 minutes whether this is a vendor pitch or a strategic conversation. Write the first 3 sentences of your expansion pitch — WITHOUT mentioning any Genpact capability. If you can't open 3 sentences without mentioning Genpact, you're leading with the wrong layer.
2. What would make Santos say "tell me more" vs. "this is just an upsell"? The difference is in whether you reference HER priorities first.
3. For each of the 3 expansion areas, identify: what did Santos say about this in public? Start there.

## checkpoint

Can your team name the CEO's stated priorities for each expansion area — from her own words, not from your service catalog? If you're starting the expansion pitch with Genpact's capabilities before Santos's priorities, you're using the wrong order.
